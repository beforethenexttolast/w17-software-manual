# Silberpfeil

**A 1/10-scale Mercedes Formula 1 car.**

*Every Silver Arrow needs a driver. This one is yours, Lola.*

---

## 1. Meet your car

Her name is **Silberpfeil** — "Silver Arrow," the nickname the racing world gave the silver Mercedes cars long ago. This one is a Mercedes W17 Formula 1 car at one-tenth scale — 3D-printed, hand-built, and made for you.

You don't drive her from the outside like a toy car. You drive her **from the inside**: a tiny camera sits in her cockpit, and its live view appears on your computer screen. Engine sound, working lights, a rear wing that really opens — the whole thing. She's a showpiece that happens to drive, not a racing toy. Treat her gently and she'll put on a show every time.

**In the box:**

- **The car.** Everything electronic lives inside her — you never need to open her up.
- **The station box.** A small box that is her radio home base. One cable connects it to your computer — that's the whole setup. [TBD-at-bench: whether the station box also ships with a small wall adapter, or runs on the one cable alone]
- **The controller.** A PlayStation-style gamepad. That's your steering wheel.
- **The key.** A small plug with a loop — her ignition key. More on the key ritual in a moment.
- **This booklet.** Lives wherever your glovebox is.

**One note:** her companion app runs on your own computer — and her helmet view runs on your phone (section 4). Both were installed and set up when she was handed over, so they're ready to go. If you ever change computers or phones, ping Vitaliy — it's a five-minute job.

## 2. Before you drive

### Charging her

She charges through a small hidden flap — the same plug as most phones (USB-C). No chargers to learn, no battery to remove.

1. **Take the key out.** That's the charging rule, every time: key out means she's completely off, and an off car is a safe-to-charge car. [TBD-at-bench: whether she visibly refuses to charge with the key in, or the key-out rule stands alone]
2. Open the flap [TBD-at-bench: flap location on the body] and plug in.
3. A little light tells you how it's going. [TBD-at-bench: charge light location and exact meanings — charging / done / something's wrong]

Charging takes about [TBD-at-bench: charge time from low to full]. A full battery gives you roughly [TBD-at-bench: drive time per charge] of driving. Use a normal phone charger [TBD-at-bench: confirmed charger recommendation and minimum wattage].

**Stay nearby while she charges** — same good habit as any rechargeable thing. Don't charge her overnight or while you're out.

### The key ritual

The key is a small plug that clicks into her [TBD-at-bench: key location on the body]. It's the whole on/off story:

- **Key out** = she's off. Safe to charge, safe to carry, safe to put away.
- **Key in** = she wakes up. Lights on, camera on, listening for you.

Make it a habit: **key out whenever you pick her up or put her away.** [TBD-at-bench: whether a spare key ships in the box]

### Where to drive

She's a Formula 1 car — she likes what the real ones like:

- **Indoors:** smooth floors. Wood, tile, smooth concrete.
- **Outdoors:** smooth, dry pavement. A quiet driveway, a smooth patio, an empty basketball court.
- **Never:** water, puddles, wet floors, sand, dirt, gravel, grass, snow, curbs, or stairs.

Keep her where you can see her, and give people and pets a polite distance while her engine is running. [TBD-at-bench: comfortable range guidance — how far is far]

## 3. Start driving

The whole routine, in order. It takes about a minute once you've done it twice.

1. **Plug the station box into your computer** with its one cable.
2. **Turn on the controller.** [TBD-at-bench: controller connection — cable or wireless pairing, and to what]
3. **Key into the car.** She wakes up: her tail light glows, and the ring above her cockpit — the halo — breathes softly for a few seconds while she gets herself sorted, then settles to a calm glow. [TBD-at-bench: final wake-up light show — she may get a proper ignition animation]

5. **The start-lights moment.** Five red lights come on across the screen... and go out. That's your cue. (It's a Formula 1 thing. It never gets old.)
6. **Wake her engine — the deliberate two-step.** She will never start with the throttle pressed, so she can never leap away from you:

   - You'll hear the starter whir, the engine fire up, and settle into an idle burble. The halo turns **teal** — her "engine running" color. She's ready.
7. **Drive.** Squeeze gently — she's set up gentle by default. Steering steers, [TBD-at-bench: throttle and brake trigger assignment]. ## 4. The helmet view — her app on your phone

The computer screen is mission control. Your phone is the fun part: open her app and you're looking out of her cockpit — her camera's live view with a slim dashboard laid over it, like wearing the driver's helmet.

**To use it:**

1. **Do the normal start routine** (section 3). The one big start button also switches on the phone link for you — no extra step.
2. **Open her app on your phone and hold it sideways** — the view is widescreen, like a cockpit. The phone and the computer find each other over the Wi-Fi on their own; there is nothing to type. [TBD-at-bench: whether the picture appears by itself or needs one tap the first time, and which Wi-Fi the phone should be on — your home Wi-Fi, her own Wi-Fi, or either]
3. **That's it.** Speed, battery, and any warnings ride along on the view, the same as on the computer screen.

It's made for passengers, too: you drive from the computer while a friend holds the phone and rides in the cockpit.

**The comforting part:** the phone is a window, never a steering wheel. It only ever *shows* things — it cannot steer her, rev her, or stop her, no matter what gets tapped. If the phone rings mid-drive or the app closes, nothing happens to her: you're driving with the controller, same as always.

## 6. When she stops by herself

First, the reassuring part: **she is careful by design.** If anything ever confuses her — the radio dips, the signal gets messy — she doesn't guess. She stops driving, straightens her wheels, closes her wing, quiets the engine, and blinks amber to tell you. **She'll park herself if she loses you.** That's her being a good car, not a broken one.

### "She's blinking amber and won't move"

She lost her connection to you for a moment — it happens behind thick walls or too far away.

1. Take your time. She's sitting safely and waiting.
2. Walk toward her a little, or bring her side of the room closer to the computer.
3. Watch the screen — when her camera picture is back and steady, she can hear you again.
4. **She deliberately won't move until you restart her engine** — even after the connection returns. Do the two-step engine start again [TBD-at-bench: whether the engine control needs to be switched off and back on first], and drive her home.

### "The halo is pulsing red"

Battery's getting low. She'll keep driving as long as you ask — she trusts you — so be kind: one calm lap home, park, key out, charge.

### "The screen froze / the app is acting up"

She has her own safety net that doesn't depend on the app: if she truly loses you, she parks herself (amber blink, as above). So don't panic — restart the app, press the start button again, and pick her back up with the two-step engine start.

If she stops by herself often in the same spot, that's a clue worth sharing — ping Vitaliy.

## 7. Care & don'ts

**Her battery** (it stays inside — you only ever see the charging flap):

- Charge while you're around, never overnight.
- After a long drive, give her ten minutes to cool before charging.
- Going away for a month or more? Don't store her freshly full or empty — a part charge is kindest. [TBD-at-bench: how to part-charge — whether the charger gets a storage setting]
- Don't leave her in a hot car or baking in a sunny window. Batteries hate heat.

**Keep her dry.** No rain, no puddles, no wet grass, no snow. If she gets dusty, a soft dry cloth or a soft brush is all she needs. Never water, never sprays.

**Don't open her up.** Everything inside is sealed, arranged, and happy. Charging happens through the flap, the key lives outside, and there is nothing inside that needs you. Opening the shell risks the bodywork — and she's rather proud of her bodywork.

**Carrying her:** key out first, then lift her by [TBD-at-bench: safe lift points] — never by the wings, the halo, or the camera.

**Around others:** her wheels spin like any car's. Keep fingers, toes, and curious pets clear while the engine is running.

## 8. If something's odd

| What's happening | Try this | Still odd? |
|---|---|---|
| Key is in, but she won't wake up | Push the key fully home. If nothing, her battery is probably empty — key out, charge her. | Ping the pit crew |
| Plugged in, but no charge light | Is the key **out**? Key out is the charging rule. Try another USB-C charger and check the plug is fully seated. | Ping the pit crew |
| The app can't find her / no picture | Check the station box cable is plugged into the computer. Close the app, open it, press start again. | Ping the pit crew |
| Controller does nothing | Reconnect the controller [TBD-at-bench: reconnect steps], then do the two-step engine start again. | Ping the pit crew |
| Whole car blinking amber, won't drive | That's her safe-stop — section 6. Get a little closer, wait for the picture, restart the engine. | Ping the pit crew |
| The gentle breathing glow never ends | Key out, count to ten, key in. | Ping the pit crew |
| Engine won't start (the two-step doesn't take) | Let go of **everything** — the trigger must be fully at rest first. Count to three, try again. | Ping the pit crew |
| Picture stutters or lags | You're probably near the edge of her comfort zone — fewer walls, less distance. | Ping the pit crew |
| She pulls to one side / steering feels wrong | Park her, key out, count to ten, key in, try again on a smooth floor. | Ping the pit crew |

**When in doubt: key out.** That's always safe, always right, and fixes more than you'd think.

And truly — ping the pit crew about anything. There is no silly question about this car. She was built for you, and that includes the help line.

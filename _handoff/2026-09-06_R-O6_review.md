# BLOCKING

### B1 — Rail B is documented at **5–6 V**, so "stall 1.9 A at 5 V" is the *lowest* of three datasheet values, not the applicable one

`O6_derivation.md`:136 (S9a never-exceed), :137 (S9b, *"**1.9 A at 5 V** — this is the number C1
exists for"*), :326-327 (§6.1 card rows), :342-345 (citation block), :367 (T11 replacement text).

The report treats Rail B as a 5 V rail throughout (:26-27 *"every derivable load figure is a 5 V
rail current"*). The project's own documents do not:

- `w17-control-fw/docs/D8_BENCH_BRINGUP.md`:55-56 — *"Confirm BEC#1 ≈ 5 V, BEC#2 ≈ **5–6 V** under a
  light load"*
- `bench-gates/BG-04_d8_bench_bringup.md`:44, :69, and the **PASS criterion** at :208 — *"BEC#1 ≈ 5 V,
  BEC#2 ≈ **5–6 V** under light load"*
- `learning-manual/03_hardware_and_electronics_basics.md`:39 — *"UBEC #2 → Rail B **5-6V** 'dirty'"*;
  `learning-manual/05_control_firmware_documentation_explained.md`:464 same.

I extracted the DS SERVO PDF myself. Its §4 row 3-4 reads, across three voltage columns:

```
  No.  Operating Voltage            5V      6V      7.4V
  3-4  Stall current (at locked)   1.9A    2.1 A   2.3A
  1-3  Operating Voltage Range  5-7.4V
```

M-PEAK's own rule is *"take the manufacturer's Max / peak column"* (:89-90). For a rail whose
documented PASS band is 5–6 V, the applicable stall figure is **2.1 A**, not 1.9 A. As written,
a never-exceed derived from 1.9 A trips on a **healthy** full-lock stall at 6 V. O-6 turns that
trip into STOP AND DIAGNOSE, the operator finds no fault, and the only exit is raising the limit —
the precise "increase until it works" loop O-6 exists to close. The same 5 V-only qualifier is
applied to the idle row (harmless: idle is 5 mA at all three voltages) and, by omission, to the
MG90S rows — TowerPro's own page (which I fetched) rates the MG90S at *"Operating voltage: 4.8V"*
with stall torque quoted at 4.8 V / 6.6 V.

**Correction.** Replace every *"stall 1.9 A at 5 V"* with **"stall 2.1 A at 6 V (1.9 A at 5 V,
2.3 A at 7.4 V) — Rail B's documented band is 5–6 V (`D8_BENCH_BRINGUP.md`:55; `BG-04`:208)"**, in
§3.2 S9a/S9b, §6.1's two S9 rows, §6.1's citation block and the T11 text. Add a bench pre-step:
record BEC#2's actual output voltage before S6–S9 and select the column from it.

### B2 — The NEVER-EXCEED column mixes the 7.4 V pack domain with the 5 V rail domain — the exact error the report names as its central block

`O6_derivation.md`:74-76 states the boundary: *"Any PSU current limit is a **7.4 V-side** limit;
every load figure derived below is a **5 V rail** current."* Then the NEVER-EXCEED column of every
substep row (:126-137) reads *"Rail A **5 A**"* / *"Rail B **5 A**"*, in a table whose limit column
L(N) is explicitly the pack-side supply setting. §6.1 repeats it verbatim at :316-327; T11 at :367.

The 5 A is a UBEC **output** rating (`HARDWARE_INVENTORY.md`:113). A pack-side supply set to a 5 A
never-exceed permits ≈ 5 A × 7.4 V ≈ 37 W, i.e. **≈ 7 A of 5 V-rail current** at η = 0.95 — 40 %
above the rail's rating, on the rail the ceiling is supposed to protect. Read the other way (a
5 V-side reader applying it pack-side) it under-sets. The report's whole §1 block (b) rests on this
boundary being uncrossable; the tables cross it silently.

**Correction.** Label the column per-domain: **"Rail A output 5 A — 5 V side, NOT a PSU setting"**.
Then either leave the pack-side never-exceed BLOCKED with the same Q2 dependency, or state the
derivable form `I_pack ≤ 5 A × 5 V / (7.4 V × η_min)` and make **η_min** an explicit owner decision
rather than an unknown that blocks everything.

### B3 — `L(N) = R(N−1) + P(N)` under-limits whenever an already-present load's peak exceeds its settled reading — and the report's own S5 data is the proof

`O6_derivation.md`:103-116 (the rule), :300-303 (§6.1 card text), :367 (T11).

`R(N−1)` is defined as *"the settled total recorded minutes earlier in the same session"* (:111-112).
From the BL-M8812EU2 datasheet I extracted, §3.3 (VDD5.0 = 5.0 V, Ta 25 °C):

```
  WLAN Unassociated                                113 / 123  mA
  HT20 MCS8 TX @ 27.5 dBm (2TX RF test)            927 / 1510 mA
```

A 12× span on one load. If S5's settled reading is taken with the module unassociated (~0.12 A),
then `L(6) = R(5) + P(blower)`. A TX burst during S6 exceeds `L(6)` by ≈ **1.4 A**. The supply
trips; O-6 mandates STOP AND DIAGNOSE; the fault is the rule, not the blower. The same holds for
the DS3235SG at every step after S9 (settled 5 mA vs 1.9–2.3 A stall), for any MG90S already
present, and for ESP32 #1 in the BT show-off build (`first_power_current_limits.md`:90, T10).

The report had this evidence in hand — its own Q5 (:251-254) enumerates the 13× range — and did not
draw the consequence. It should have.

**Correction.** State the rule as

```
  L(N) = max( R(N-1),  Σ_{k<N} P(k) )  +  P(N)
```

i.e. a settled reading may substitute for a load only where **no** cited peak exists; any load with
a cited peak contributes its **peak** at every later substep. Add one sentence: *"a settled reading
is never a bound on a bursty load."*

### B4 — M-PEAK's inrush carve-out is unbounded, unfalsifiable, and reasons on the wrong side of the UBEC

`O6_derivation.md`:97-101, repeated verbatim in the card text at :294-298.

**(a) Wrong domain.** C1 is *"across **Rail B** (+5V ↔ GND)"* and C2 is *"across the **WS2812 strip**
5V ↔ GND at the first LED"* (`w17-pdb-build-and-connector-guide.md`:119-120) — both on the **5 V**
side, downstream of the switching UBECs. The supply is on the **7.4 V** side (the report's own
:74-76). What the supply sees at a connect is UBEC input capacitance and soft-start behaviour, not
C1/C2 charging. The inrush argument silently crosses the boundary the report declared uncrossable.

**(b) No criterion.** *"Momentarily enters, then leaves"* (:100-101) has no time bound, and neither
does the stop wording it leans on (`first_power_current_limits.md`:119, *"the supply **sits** in
constant-current"*). Order of magnitude: 1000 µF to 5 V over a 2 ms soft start is
`i = C·dV/dt = 2.5 A` on the 5 V side — half the rail rating. At a low starting limit the supply can
hold the rail down so the UBEC never completes soft-start, which presents **exactly** as "sitting"
in CC. The operator then has a stop with no way to separate limit-too-low from a short, and the
only exit is raising the limit.

**Correction.** Give the carve-out a number the owner rules — e.g. *"CC persisting > N ms after a
connect, or CC that does not clear before the rail reaches nominal, is a STOP"* — and add it to §5
as an owner question. Do not ship "momentarily" as the discriminator between healthy and fault.

### B5 — Q4 (and Q8's back-reference) proposes an **unbounded** ramp, which is not consistent with O-6

`O6_derivation.md`:246-250 — *"the limit raised in the supply's smallest increments **until it leaves
constant-current**"*; :261-262 — Q8 *"characterised the same way as Q4"*.

The staircase authorises this **only** for the empty-rail case, and pairs it with a hard stop:
`first_power_current_limits.md`:103-105 — *"Raise the limit only until the supply comes out of
constant-current mode **with nothing connected** … A harness that cannot come out of CC with nothing
on the rails **has a fault — stop, do not raise the limit**."* Q4 extends the technique to **loaded**
substeps (S1, S2, and via Q8 to S7/S8) and drops the ceiling: the only bound left is the rail's 5 A,
and 5 A into two D1-mini ESP32 boards destroys them long before any rail limit is approached.

Both sides, as the brief asks. **For:** it is a characterisation of an unknown quiescent draw, not a
response to a fault; the "trip" is the intended CC-limited start, understood before it happens; it is
step 2's own technique. **Against:** what makes step 2 legitimate is precisely its ceiling, and Q4
removes it; operationally "raise in small increments until it works" is the literal text O-6 forbids;
and no independent criterion tells the operator that a shorted board would also "leave CC", higher up.
**Verdict: not consistent as written.** The ceiling is the safety content and it is missing.

**Correction.** Q4 and Q8 must each name a per-substep **ramp ceiling above which the step is a fault
regardless**. Since no cited figure exists for the MH-ET board, that ceiling *is* the owner decision
the question should be asking for — the question as written asks for permission instead of a number.

### B6 — The Worldsemi negative is false against the current WS2812B revision, which does publish a per-channel operating current

`O6_derivation.md`:53 — *"**fetched (Worldsemi WS2812B V1.0, Jan 2016) — contains NO supply-current
figure.** Its Electrical Characteristics table gives only 'Input current II VI=VDD/VSS … ±1 µA'"*;
:129 — *"Worldsemi's datasheet supplies **nothing** here"*. This negative is the sole justification for
deriving the strip current from firmware alone.

I fetched **both** revisions and extracted them.

- **Jan-2016 V1.0** (SparkFun mirror, *"WS2812B Datasheet and Specifications … Jan,2016 V1.0"*): its
  *"RGB IC characteristic parameter"* table has columns Emitting color / Model / Wavelength / Luminous
  intensity / **Voltage** — no current column. The report's negative is true **for this revision only**.
- **Mar-2017** (`WORLDSEMI CO., LIMITED — WS2812B Specifications — Mar-2017`, `world-semi.com` footer on
  every page): its *"LED Characteristics"* table reads

```
  Color   Wavelength(nm)  Intensity(mcd)  Operating Current(mA)  Operating Voltage(V)
  RED     620-630         550-700         16                     1.8-2.2
  GREEN   515-530         1100-1400       16                     2.8-3.1
  BLUE    465-475         200-400         16                     2.9-3.2
```

The consequence is favourable — 16 mA/channel **bounds below** the code model's 20 mA/channel, so
560 mA at cap 180 is conservative by ~25 % (2 × 16 × 119/255 × 30 = 448 mA) — but the report asserts a
universal manufacturer silence that is not true, and in doing so hides the one manufacturer figure
that could corroborate its model.

**Correction.** Quote the Mar-2017 *LED Characteristics* row in §2 and §6.1, state which revision each
claim applies to, and say that 16 mA/channel bounds the firmware's 20 mA/channel from below.

### B7 — 560 mA is called "the honest real-number bound", but the model rests on an uncited constant and omits a real term

`O6_derivation.md`:18, :129, :184 (*"<- the honest real-number bound"*), :216 (*"[code model,
un-truncated upper bound]"*), :319, :367.

Two problems with calling it a bound on the **strip**:

1. The model's 20 mA/channel comes from a **code comment** — `LightRenderer.hpp`:205, *"WS2812 ~ 20 mA
   /channel at full duty"* — not from any manufacturer document. That is the same class of uncited
   family figure the report refuses at :415-421 (the espboards.dev "80 mA" for the MH-ET board, the
   reseller "MG90S ~400 mA stall"). The report applies its own rule inconsistently, in favour of the
   one figure it wanted to keep.
2. Neither Worldsemi revision publishes the WS2812B's **controller/quiescent supply current**, which is
   real (each pixel's logic draws current at zero duty) and is **absent from the model entirely**. The
   *"Input current II ±1 µA"* the report quotes is a DIN-pin leakage spec, not a supply current — the
   report labels it correctly but then treats its absence as "no term exists".

So 560 mA is an upper bound on the **emitter** model, not on the **strip**.

**Correction.** Relabel every occurrence: *"model upper bound on emitter current; excludes each pixel's
controller quiescent draw, which Worldsemi does not publish"*, and carry the missing term as a named
unquantified adder (or a bench item) rather than silently omitting it from the Rail-A floor.
